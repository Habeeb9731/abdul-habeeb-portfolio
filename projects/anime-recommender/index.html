AnimeRec

A hybrid anime recommender built on 23.2M real list interactions. It handles brand-new visitors and titles nobody has rated yet, and was tested on a held-out time window.

Overview

Popularity lists ignore your taste. Collaborative filtering ignores new shows.

AnimeRec ranks every eligible title for each visitor. It leans on what similar viewers enjoyed, then shifts smoothly toward synopsis and metadata signals as a title’s history falls to zero. Answer three quick questions and your top 10 appears instantly, with no retraining and a reason attached to each pick.

Challenge & solution

What made it hard, and how I approached it.

The challenge

An evaluation that could be trusted

  • Collaborative filtering cannot rank a title nobody has watched yet.
  • The newer dataset looked ideal, but 97.0% of 9.8M sampled “interaction dates” were really air dates or placeholders.
  • Anime viewing follows the airing season, so a plain popularity baseline makes any model look better than it is.
The solution

Real timestamps, a harder baseline

  • Switched to a crawl with per-user update times, then joined synopses from the newer dataset by anime ID.
  • Split globally by time and ranked the full 10,720-title catalog, with no negative sampling and the test window scored only once.
  • Each title blends collaborative and content scores by how much history it has, and results are reported against recent popularity too.
Key features

Built around what people actually need.

Instant taste profile

Pick genres and a few favourites, and new users are folded into the model on the spot, without retraining.

A reason for every pick

Recommendations say why they are there, like “Because you liked One Punch Man”, and every title opens a detail card.

Room for new titles

A title with no viewing history can still be scored from its synopsis, genres, studio, type and year.

Responsive

Made for browsing on a phone

On small screens the side rail becomes a floating bottom pill, and the picks stay within thumb reach.

  • Floating bottom navigation on phones
  • Answers are remembered in the browser, with no account
  • Sequels of titles you already picked stay hidden by default

How it works

Under the hood.

  • Data23.2M interactions from 100,000 sampled users, after logged filters removed bulk imports and bot-like accounts.
  • ModelsEASE and ALS for collaborative filtering, and all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence embeddings with metadata for content.
  • HybridPer title, alpha = n / (n + κ) moves the score from collaborative to content signals as its history n falls to zero.
  • DemoA Streamlit app in Docker behind a Cloudflare Tunnel, serving the same model that was evaluated.

Takeaway

What I learned.

The most useful step came before any model: auditing the timestamps. It stopped me from reporting a time split that was not really one. It also taught me to publish the less flattering numbers. The hybrid only matches plain EASE overall, and new titles rarely reach a top 10.
