Overview

Any forecast can look good in hindsight. depower commits to its numbers before the market does.

Germany’s wholesale electricity prices for each hour of tomorrow are set in an auction at 12:00 today. Every morning before that, depower forecasts all 24 prices with a likely range for each hour, using grid data from SMARD and ECMWF weather forecasts. Each forecast is logged once and can’t be overwritten. When the real prices are published, it gets scored in public. Live forecasts began on 3 November 2026, for delivery the next day.

Challenge & solution

What made it hard, and how I approached it.

The challenge

A forecast is only honest if it uses what was known at the time

  • One value published after the auction is enough to make a backtest look better than the model could ever be live.
  • SMARD keeps no history of when values were published, and some forecast series only seem to appear after the auction closes.
  • A good error score proves little on its own. It needs simple baselines to beat, and a clear line between simulated and real results.
The solution

Make leaking hard, then publish the misses

  • Every cleaned row carries an available-at time, and features can only read through a view that hides anything newer than the 11:30 cutoff.
  • Tests overwrite post-cutoff values with absurd numbers and confirm no feature changes. An audit of all 23,136 real feature rows found none past its cutoff.
  • Each log entry stores the hash of the one before and CI fails if a forecast changes. The dashboard shows backtest and live results separately.

How it works

Under the hood.

  • DataSMARD (Bundesnetzagentur) for prices, demand and generation, Open-Meteo for ECMWF weather at six locations, and an offline holidays package.
  • ModelLightGBM quantile regression for P10, P50 and P90 on 72 features, retrained monthly in the backtest, with conformal calibration by hour.
  • PipelinePython and DuckDB. Raw responses are versioned, quality checks gate every forecast, and a late or incomplete run publishes nothing.
  • FrontendNext.js static export with shadcn/ui and Recharts, in English and German.
  • DeploymentDocker containers on a Hetzner server, run by systemd timers at 10:20, 10:45 and 11:10 Berlin time. GitHub Actions runs CI.

Takeaway

What I learned.

My first backtest’s P10–P90 ranges covered only 58.5% of real prices when they should have covered 80%. Calibration brought that to 77.9%, but I chose that fix after seeing the result, so the number may be slightly flattering, and the project says so. That taught me a forecast earns trust less through its error score than by showing what it could see, where it fails and what a simple baseline would have done.
