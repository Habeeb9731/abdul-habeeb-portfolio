Overview

Two plants, two soils, one terrarium. EDEN keeps both alive — and shows you how.

EDEN is a smart terrarium with two independent watering zones. An ESP32 sensor hub reads soil moisture, temperature, humidity, light and the water reservoir, and drives two pumps, a fan and a grow light. The web dashboard turns those readings into a calm, glanceable view and lets you take over manual control.

Challenge & solution

What made it hard, and how I approached it.

The challenge

Real hardware, real constraints

  • Two plants with different watering needs in the same enclosure.
  • A small sensor hub on a local network, with request timeouts under load.
  • Automation that people can trust and override.
The solution

Simple, transparent, offline-first

  • Independent per-zone soil moisture and pumps, each with its own thresholds.
  • A dependency-free PWA that polls the hub directly and handles a disconnected device gracefully.
  • Auto / Manual control for every actuator, with plain-language explanations of why automation is acting.
Key features

Built around what people actually need.

Plant health

A single health score plus humidity, soil and reservoir levels on the home screen.

Systems control

Auto or manual control of Pump A, Pump B, the fan and the grow light.

Works offline

Installable PWA with a demo mode that simulates the terrarium when the device is unreachable.

Responsive

Designed phone-first

The dashboard was designed for a phone next to the terrarium, then extended to a two-column desktop layout.

  • Thumb-reachable floating navigation
  • Health panel readable at a glance
  • Installable as an app on the home screen

How it works

Under the hood.

  • HardwareESP32 sensor hub with soil moisture, temperature, humidity, light and reservoir sensors; pumps, fan and grow light.
  • Device firmwareC++ on the ESP32, plus a touchscreen interface on the device itself.
  • DashboardVanilla HTML, CSS and JavaScript — zero dependencies, zero CDN calls, installable as a PWA.
  • TeamBuilt by Team 17 for the Interactive Systems course at Saarland University.

Takeaway

What I learned.

Building for real hardware changes priorities: graceful failure, clear status and trustworthy automation matter more than features. Working in a team also meant agreeing early on the data contract between the device and the dashboard.
