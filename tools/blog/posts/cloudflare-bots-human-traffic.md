On The Verge's Decoder podcast, Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince made a big prediction, [as reported by The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/cloudflare-matthew-prince-bots-1000x-human-traffic-verge): "Five years from now, we think that automated traffic will be 1,000 times human traffic online."

Cloudflare sits in front of more than 20% of the web, so it sees a lot. By Prince's account, his team said in May that bots had already passed human traffic, earlier than he had predicted.

## Why this matters

The web's business model assumes people. As Prince put it, "Bots don't click on ads." So Cloudflare is working with Coinbase and Stripe to revive HTTP 402, which would let sites charge AI agents a fraction of a penny per page. From 15 September, free Cloudflare customers also block Google from using their sites for AI training by default, and owners can turn that off.

## Reasons for caution

- The 1,000x figure is a forecast, and no method or underlying data is published in the article.
- By his own account, his earlier predictions were off.
- Cloudflare sells products for managing bots, so it has an interest in this story.
- Nilay Patel pushed back on whether micropayments give people a reason to publish, and Yahoo's Jim Lanzone has argued they won't be enough.

## My read

I'd ignore the exact number and keep the direction. Even a fraction of that would change how sites plan capacity, measure audiences and pay for content. If you run a site, look at your own logs. How much of your traffic is already automated, and what is it costing you? That answer is much more useful than any forecast from a CEO.
