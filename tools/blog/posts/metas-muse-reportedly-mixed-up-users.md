Meta employees have raised concerns that Muse, the company's AI agent, has been mixing up conversations across different accounts and exposing private user data. The concerns come from a [Reuters report based on internal company posts, covered by TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/metas-ai-agent-muse-is-mixing-up-conversations-and-exposing-private-data-report-says/).

## What employees describe

According to the report, the problems went beyond one odd reply. Employees said Muse surfaced messages about childcare requests they never made, career advice they never asked for, product orders they never placed, and bank account balances that didn't belong to them.

One employee said Muse added an unrelated message to a chat about weather and tide information. The message read: "BTW, two bank updates, your Capital One balance is $2722.58, your Charles Schwab balance is $5899.57."

In another case, an employee using Muse on a personal phone got a reminder to reply to a message from her sister on Signal, the encrypted messaging app. She wrote in her internal post: "This seems like an extreme privacy violation. I definitely did not grant it access to other apps, or recall being asked about this."

## Meta's response

A Meta spokesperson told TechCrunch that employee testing is part of product development and is meant to produce feedback that leads to safety and privacy protections. The spokesperson also said employees use different versions of Muse than the public, so they are often testing features and behavior that aren't available elsewhere.

Meta has not said whether the versions employees described were affected, or whether the public version has the same behavior. The report does not say how many accounts were involved, and the company has not said what it has changed.

Muse launched in September and has more than 10 million downloads, according to the report. TechCrunch describes it as one of the fastest-growing apps on record.

## My read

The reported examples are specific. Each one involves a different user's data, and the bank balances are the kind of detail people would not expect an assistant to show them. Meta's answer is that this is what testing is for. That is a fair description of why companies run staff tests, but it doesn't say whether the problem is fixed or how widespread it is.

If you use Muse, it's worth checking which apps and accounts you have connected to it, and whether you have shared any financial information with it. Until Meta gives more detail on the cause and the scope, the reports should be treated as serious allegations from inside the company, not as a confirmed account of how many people were affected.
