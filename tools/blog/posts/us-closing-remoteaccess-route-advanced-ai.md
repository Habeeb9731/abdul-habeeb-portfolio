The US Commerce Department is changing its export rules to close what it calls a loophole. Foreign rivals could previously rent access to restricted high-end AI chips from data centers in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brazil. [Ars Technica's Ashley reports](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/us-moves-to-cut-chinas-remote-access-to-ai-chips/) that the department named China, Russia, and Iran as the countries it was worried about.

## What the rule does

The rule takes effect 90 days after it is posted to the Federal Register. The article does not say when that posting happens.

Companies headquartered in the five countries that are judged to "present a higher-than-normal risk of diversion" to China, Russia, or Iran will need a license to transfer high volumes of advanced chips. Licensed firms must perform "robust due diligence" and file monthly reports that name all parties, their parent companies, and the final destination of the chips.

There is one carve-out. Computing power "exclusively used for commercial recommender systems" is exempt.

The South China Morning Post, as quoted in the article, reported that any advanced chips made for a Chinese firm are now assumed to be restricted, and factories must ask the US government for a license before shipping them.

Companies that break the rules could face new import restrictions, stricter license requirements, a lower status in the export control regime, or changes to entity-list designations. The Commerce Department lists these as possible consequences. It has not said which ones it would use.

## The Nvidia backdrop

The rule comes after a scandal involving Nvidia. The article reports that CEO Jensen Huang resigned after admitting to an affair with Alice Huang, who is suspected of serving as a Chinese spy. China denies that she is a spy. Congress is probing whether she used the relationship to help her former company, Megaspeed, become one of Nvidia's biggest customers and then smuggled restricted chips into China. Megaspeed, a Singaporean data center firm, denies that smuggling happened and has started its own internal probe.

CBS News reported that US investigators allege Alice Huang used industry contacts and shell companies to access 240,000 banned chips. Trump has pushed back on the spy claim: "Nobody's proven that she's a spy," he said. "She may not be a spy."

## Officials disagree

The article describes a split inside the administration. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent reportedly pushed for curbs, while Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick reportedly favored looser controls. Trump said Lutnick was right to delay blacklisting Megaspeed. The New York Times reported that enforcement of the stricter rules would fall to the Commerce Department, which is the agency Bessent has criticized.

## China's response

China is unlikely to welcome the rule. Hong Yuan, a researcher at the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences, told the People's Daily that China will likely see it as political: "The issue is not the cause of tighter controls, but a political catalyst." Analysts told the South China Morning Post the policy could "accelerate Chinese companies' shift to domestic AI chips."

A senior Trump official, speaking anonymously to the New York Times, said Trump "did not expect countermeasures from China in response." Trump is due at an economic summit in China this week, and the article says the new curbs could complicate his talks with Xi Jinping.

## My read

The rule closes a route that the article says was legal until now, so the policy direction is clear. What is unclear is enforcement. The article shows the rule was shaped by an internal fight, and it is not clear how hard the Commerce Department will push it. The Alice Huang allegations also rest on investigators' claims, and the Chinese government and Trump both dispute the spy part.

If you rent GPU capacity in those five countries, the 90-day window is the time to check whether your provider's headquarters or your customers fall under the license requirement. The article does not list which companies are affected beyond Megaspeed.
